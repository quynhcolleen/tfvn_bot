## National payments mission and commander's intent

The TrapNet National Payments Mobilization Program defines the long-term evolution of Trap Coin into **Trap Financial Network (TrapNet)**: a national-scale fictional financial system presented through Discord. Rather than one balance field with several commands, the target model contains independently accountable institutions that authorize, clear, settle, reconcile, supervise, and audit one another.

The project would serve as an educational laboratory for accounting, distributed systems, fault tolerance, formal verification, cryptographic auditability, economic simulation, and disaster recovery. It must remain a fictional, non-redeemable community system.

The target institutional model is intentionally comprehensive:

- A Trap Central Bank controls explicit base-money mint and burn operations.
- Every participating Discord guild may become a fictional monetary jurisdiction, issue
  a native guild currency, and enter formally versioned foreign-exchange treaties
  with other guilds.
- Member banks maintain customer deposit liabilities, savings, credit, and statements.
- A card scheme separates issuing, acquiring, authorization, clearing, and settlement.
- A clearing house nets obligations between institutions.
- A real-time gross settlement system transfers central-bank reserves.
- A fictional regulator, auditor, dispute court, and deposit insurer supervise the system.
- Blackjack and Poker use casino escrow and normal ledger transactions instead of editing balances directly.
- A deterministic economic digital twin can simulate inflation, bank runs, fraud, liquidity crises, and recovery.

Prospective mission objectives are:

1. Preserve the integrity of base money, deposit liabilities, reserves, holds, and settlement positions.
2. Provide deterministic and auditable payment finality across mutually distrustful institutions.
3. Prevent unauthorized issuance, debit, capture, reversal, or history mutation.
4. Remain safe under duplicate delivery, process failure, region loss, clock skew, and bounded Byzantine behavior.
5. Produce independently reproducible accounting, operational, security, and assurance evidence.
6. Minimize data collection and keep real financial credentials and unnecessary PII outside the system.
7. Separate presentation, authorization, accounting, risk analysis, settlement, oversight, and recovery authorities.
8. Support deterministic simulation of monetary policy, financial crime, systemic stress, and institutional resolution.

## National scope and hard safety boundaries

Any future implementation must remain within the approved fictional and non-safety-critical system boundary.

- Do not accept or store real payment-card numbers, CVVs, bank credentials, government identifiers, addresses, or other unnecessary PII.
- Do not use a real card-network issuer identification number or create an identifier that could be mistaken for a real payment card.
- Do not claim PCI DSS, banking, aviation, or other regulatory compliance based on code or documentation alone.
- Do not make Trap Coin redeemable, cash-convertible, or pegged to Bitcoin or fiat currency under this specification. Any such proposal would be a separate system and specification requiring explicit supersession of this scope, legal authority, and independent review.
- Do not run unrequested CPU/GPU workloads on member devices. Any mining feature should be an opt-in virtual simulator.
- Do not invent cryptographic primitives. Use reviewed libraries and established algorithms behind replaceable interfaces.
- Do not send card secrets, PINs, encryption keys, or sensitive credentials through Discord messages, modals, or components.
- Do not allow Discord availability, latency, identity, or message delivery to control any function whose failure could injure a person.
- Do not treat an AML alert or model score as proof of wrongdoing, and do not let it autonomously alter balances, access, reputation, or external reporting.

## Settlement operating doctrine

The prospective system boundary includes the central-bank ledger, member-bank ledgers, clearing, RTGS settlement, the Trap Card scheme and switch, policy services, the read-only AML Decision-Support and Case-Management Subsystem, cryptographic evidence, reconciliation, oversight, recovery, simulation, and replaceable user-interface adapters.

Discord remains outside the trusted financial core. It may collect a payment intent and display a result, but the core must independently authenticate the actor, validate the complete transaction, enforce policy, and produce the authoritative outcome.

Prospective operating modes are:

| Mode | Permitted behavior | Entry and exit authority |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `OFFLINE` | No external or financial processing; offline signed diagnostic tooling only | Dual-control operator procedure |
| `STARTING` | Verify configuration, keys, journal, quorum, time, and projections; no settlement | Automated checks plus operator approval |
| `NORMAL` | Full authorized transaction processing | Quorum-certified transition |
| `DEGRADED` | Continue only capabilities permitted by a versioned degradation matrix; mark stale projections | Automated safety policy or quorum |
| `SETTLEMENT_HALT` | Authenticated read, evidence preservation, and control-plane recovery only; no economic mutation or new finality | Automatic invariant control; release requires maker-checker and quorum |
| `RECOVERY` | Restore, replay, reconcile, and shadow-validate; no normal traffic | Incident commander plus quorum evidence |
| `EMERGENCY` | Only predeclared, expiring operations that preserve quorum, valid-key, accounting, and settlement-halt controls | Threshold authority with retrospective review |

Mode changes would be immutable, signed business events. A user-interface message could never change a core mode directly.

Replica lifecycle states would be `BOOTSTRAPPING`, `NON_VOTING_SHADOW`, `VOTING`, and `QUARANTINED`. A shadow or quarantined replica could not contribute to a commit certificate. Loss of an AML model would be a `DEGRADED` condition and would not block deterministic core settlement. Loss of quorum, accounting integrity, or trustworthy signing authority would require `SETTLEMENT_HALT`.

`EOD_CLOSE` is a scoped business-process state, not a global service mode. An institution or business date may close and reconcile while unrelated eligible 24×7 processing continues. Emergency authority can reduce service or preserve evidence but cannot override quorum, valid-key, accounting, or settlement-halt controls.

## Proposed National Service Charter

These numerical targets are unapproved design inputs, not evidence of PFMI or other conformance. Integrity properties remain requirements rather than percentages and take precedence over latency and availability. A protective halt counts as unavailability, not as an integrity failure.

For these candidate objectives:

- An **acknowledged commit** is a transition durably recorded by the required voting quorum with a valid commit certificate before the success response is emitted.
- A **recovery interval** begins at the earliest recorded automated detection or confirmed observation that a critical service cannot process safely, regardless of when an incident is formally declared. It ends only after state-root verification, journal replay, reconciliation, restored quorum and keys, controlled probes, and authorized service resumption.
- An **eligible request** is a syntactically valid, authenticated, policy-permitted request received at the core boundary. Rejections caused by system faults, dependency failures, capacity exhaustion, or protective halts count against availability; invalid or unauthorized requests do not.
- Latency is measured server-side from accepted core request to the stated response boundary over an approved calendar-month window. Timeouts and system errors are unsuccessful observations, not discarded samples.
- The **approved projected peak** is the signed workload in capacity plan `CAP-001`, including message mix, account contention, batch load, failure load, and growth assumptions.
- Projection checkpoint RPO, projection freshness, and full rebuild time are separate indicators; no projection loss changes authoritative journal RPO.

## Central Accounting Canon and candidate invariants

The authoritative financial state should be an append-only, double-entry journal. Read models and cached balances are disposable projections.

Candidate invariants for a separately approved ledger design include:

1. Every posted transaction balances to zero per currency.
2. Only an explicit, threshold-approved mint or burn transaction changes total supply.
3. Posted entries are immutable; corrections are linked reversals or compensating entries.
4. One idempotency key produces at most one economic result.
5. Each state transition is atomic; an interrupted multi-stage workflow remains in an explicit, recoverable state and never creates an unjournaled economic effect.
6. Available funds exclude active holds.
7. Cumulative captured value never exceeds cumulative valid authorization, including separately approved increments.
8. Negative balances require an explicit credit facility.
9. Consent binds the payer, recipient, amount, currency, purpose, nonce, and expiry.
10. No institution can debit an account it does not control.
11. Settlement finality never moves backward.
12. Replaying the authoritative journal produces identical balances and state hashes.
13. Administrators cannot edit money; they can only initiate authorized, auditable transactions.
14. A reconciliation mismatch quarantines affected operations instead of silently repairing history.
15. Each institution balances independently; base money reconciles to central-bank issuance, while deposit liabilities reconcile to the issuing banks and are not blindly added to reserves as another copy of supply.

Possible ledger features include:

- Integer accounting in a smallest fictional unit such as `trapsat`, with no relationship to Bitcoin satoshis.
- Bitemporal records containing both business-effective time and system-recorded time.
- Per-account sequence numbers and optimistic concurrency control.
- Transactional inbox/outbox processing with at-least-once delivery and exactly-once economic effect.
- Rebuildable customer balances, statements, reports, and leaderboards.
- End-of-day closing, trial balance, suspense accounts, reconciliation, and signed settlement reports.

A national-scale monetary model could explicitly separate monetary layers:

- **Base Trap Coin:** central-bank liabilities used as physical-equivalent currency and institutional reserves. Its supply equals approved minting minus burning.
- **Bank deposit Trap Coin:** liabilities of member banks to customers. Lending may create deposits, so this aggregate must be reported separately from base money.
- **Consolidated reports:** eliminate inter-institution assets and liabilities instead of summing both sides and double-counting money.

The current bot uses MongoDB. This roadmap does not authorize a migration. If a ledger redesign is explicitly approved, its storage model should receive a separate architecture decision. A transactional relational ledger with MongoDB retained for read projections is one possible design, not a current requirement.

## Guild monetary federation and TrapFX

The federation could treat each Discord guild as a fictional monetary jurisdiction
with its own central bank, treasury, monetary charter, inflation arguments, and
issuance authority. A guild could call its unit `TC`, `BANANA`, `ORBIT`, or anything
else allowed by policy. Display codes are cosmetic; the real identity is always
issuer-qualified:

```text
CurrencyRef {
  issuer_guild_id,
  currency_id,
  display_code,
  display_name,
  minor_unit_exponent,
  charter_version
}

(Guild A, tc) ≠ (Guild B, tc)
```

Thus, owning `100 TC` in Guild A does not imply owning `100 TC` in Guild B. A
receipt should say something like `100 A/TC → 73 B/TC`, with both guild names and
canonical currency references visible. This is foreign exchange between fictional
community monies, not movement of one global balance between server namespaces.

### Local monetary sovereignty and controlled issuance

Each guild could operate a **Guild Monetary Authority (GMA)**. Server owners would
appoint issuer roles, treasury operators, and independent approvers. The GMA could:

- Publish the currency name, ticker, smallest unit, icon, and monetary charter.
- Create a genesis allocation to a disclosed guild treasury.
- Propose minting, burning, treasury grants, faucet budgets, staking rewards,
  mining rewards, taxes, fees, subsidies, and emergency liquidity.
- Set issuance ceilings, daily flow limits, timelocks, approval thresholds, and
  automatic expiry for unused authority.
- Publish supply, treasury, reserve, inflation, and distribution reports.
- Retire a currency through an orderly redemption-in-fiction and archive process.

The journal would record a sober `CurrencyIssuanceAuthorized` event followed by
balanced entries:

```text
Debit   Guild A monetary-issuance contra account    1,000,000 A/TC
Credit  Guild A treasury account                    1,000,000 A/TC
```

No administrator would type a new balance into a user record. A mint request
would carry its proposal ID, amount, destination treasury, purpose code, charter
version, activation time, expiry, initiator, independent approvals, and audit
evidence. Burning would debit a controlled treasury or redemption account and
credit the issuance contra account. The supply dashboard would be derived from
the journal and independently reconciled, because trusting a mutable
`total_supply` field would not meet the target assurance standard.

For institutional completeness, each guild could schedule:

- Monetary-policy committee meetings with proposals and recorded dissent.
- Inflation targets measured only inside the fictional economy.
- Open-market operations in fictional guild bonds.
- Reserve requirements for guild-chartered member banks.
- A discount window and lender-of-last-resort facility.
- Currency redenomination after a deliberately severe fictional hyperinflation
  scenario.

### Bilateral exchange treaties

Guilds should not become exchangeable merely because one administrator listed a
rate. Guild A and Guild B would negotiate a versioned **FX Corridor Treaty**:

```text
FxCorridor {
  corridor_id,
  sell_currency,
  buy_currency,
  settlement_model,
  quote_sources[],
  allowed_participant_policy,
  reserve_accounts[],
  fee_schedule,
  exposure_limits,
  price_bands,
  circuit_breakers,
  dispute_policy,
  guild_a_approval,
  guild_b_approval,
  effective_from,
  expires_at,
  version,
  status
}
```

One side proposes; the other may counter, approve, or reject. Amendments create a
new version and cannot rewrite old trades. Suspension stops new quotes but does
not erase balances, receipts, or already-final exchange. Termination defines how
unused reserves, open orders, fees, and disputes are closed without trapping user
funds in an unexpected intermediate state.

Membership policy must also be explicit. A sensible default would let an account
retain a guild-issued balance after the user leaves that guild, while requiring
current eligibility and acceptance of both charters before new trading or spending.
Guild departure would never silently confiscate, burn, or teleport currency.

### Approved exchange venue models

TrapFX could support several mutually exclusive venue types per corridor:

1. **Bilateral peg:** the two GMAs publish an exact rational rate such as
   `73 B/TC for 100 A/TC`, with a bounded spread and periodic review.
2. **Treasury dealer:** each side deposits inventory into segregated reserves and
   signs time-limited bid/ask quotes.
3. **Batch auction:** orders accumulate until a scheduled clearing time; one
   reproducible clearing price maximizes executable volume under price limits.
4. **Order book:** users place price-time-priority limit orders backed by holds;
   cancel, partial fill, expiry, and self-trade prevention are explicit states.
5. **Automated market maker:** a fictional pool applies a published integer-only
   curve, fee, rounding rule, and slippage bound. Pool shares remain fictional and
   promise no investment return.
6. **Simulation oracle:** a deterministic economic model publishes replayable
   research prices but receives no settlement credentials.

There would be no universal “the exchange rate.” A quote identifies its venue and
time. A best-execution router could compare eligible quotes, but it must not split
an order unless the user authorizes the resulting venues, fees, and minimum output.
Rates use integer fractions or fixed-point decimals; binary floating-point is
excluded from posted financial calculations.

Triangular routes such as `A/TC → B/BANANA → C/ORBIT` could be allowed only when
every leg has a valid quote and the whole route commits atomically or is protected
by explicit holds and recoverable settlement states. Arbitrage is a normal market
signal in the simulation, not automatically an AML offense. Failed routes must not
leave the user with an accidental halfway currency unless that possibility was
explicitly accepted.

### Reserve-backed four-leg settlement

The default safe model would be reserve-backed exchange. If a user sells Guild A
currency to receive Guild B currency, the economic transaction has at least four
principal legs plus fees:

```text
Guild A books                         Guild B books
─────────────                         ─────────────
Debit  User A/TC                      Debit  FX reserve B/TC
Credit FX reserve A/TC                Credit User B/TC

          + separately balanced fee entries per currency
          + one shared exchange ID and final commit certificate
```

The A currency still exists—it moved into the corridor reserve. The B currency
also still exists—it moved out of its reserve. No supply changed. If the B reserve
cannot deliver the quoted amount, the trade rejects or waits in an explicit state;
TrapFX may not cover the deficit through undeclared issuance.

An alternative **monetary conversion facility** could intentionally burn A and
mint B. That is not ordinary FX. It changes two monetary supplies and therefore
requires separate threshold issuance decisions from both GMAs, explicit supply
effects in the quote, and stronger limits. Calling it “exchange” would not make
the inflation disappear.

Atomic settlement could require a multi-currency ledger transaction or a formally
specified hold/prepare/commit protocol. The design must survive a crash after every
message boundary, repeated Discord interactions, stale quotes, delayed settlement,
and retries. A transaction identifier is not enough; the same idempotency key with
different content must be rejected as a conflict.

### Economic controls and failure cases

The simulation should make bad policy possible but accounting corruption
impossible. A guild may deliberately inflate its currency, subsidize new members,
tax casinos, or peg to another guild; dashboards should expose the consequences.
Useful fictional controls and metrics include:

- Monetary base, circulating supply, treasury balance, velocity, concentration,
  Gini coefficient, price indexes, issuance schedule, and realized inflation.
- Corridor reserves, utilization, bid/ask spread, depth, slippage, turnover,
  settlement failures, and concentration by counterparty.
- Per-guild and per-user daily FX limits, open-order exposure, treasury loss limits,
  and maximum price movement before a circuit breaker.
- Stress tests for bank runs, hyperinflation, illiquid pegs, reserve exhaustion,
  flash crashes, oracle divergence, coordinated runs, guild deletion, and attempted
  market capture through a deliberately trivial offer.
- Public policy history and signed proofs that past rates, supply, and trades were
  not edited after the fact.

Important edge cases include identical tickers, renamed/deleted guilds, a user who
belongs to only one side, precision mismatch, dust, rounding direction, fee-on-fee,
partial fills, expired quotes, frozen corridors, depleted reserves, simultaneous
mint and trade, charter upgrades, issuer key loss, Discord outage, economic replay,
and migration from the current global Trap Coin balance.

## Seven-Region Settlement Quorum

“Backup” and “voting replica” are different roles. Offline backups cannot participate in consensus, and more copies do not automatically protect against a shared software defect.

The counts below are illustrative fault-model arithmetic, not a consensus design. Safety and liveness depend on the selected protocol, authenticated communication, synchrony assumptions, honest-node locking rules, durable state, epoch handling, and formally safe reconfiguration. Matching signatures alone do not establish Byzantine consensus.

| Failure model | Active replicas | Illustrative quorum | Nominal bound under documented protocol assumptions |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Crash-fault consensus | 3 | 2 | 1 unavailable replica |
| Crash-fault consensus | 5 | 3 | 2 unavailable replicas |
| Byzantine consensus | 4 | 3 | 1 arbitrary replica |
| Proposed high-assurance Byzantine consensus | 7 | 5 | 2 arbitrary replicas |

The prospective high-assurance topology is:

- Seven active Byzantine-fault-tolerant replicas.
- A 5-of-7 commit certificate produced by the selected formally specified protocol for every final decision.
- At least three geographically and administratively isolated non-voting recovery nodes, each holding immutable signed snapshots and journal copies.
- Independent fault domains for compute, storage, credentials, networking, and deployment.
- A deterministic state machine with versioned commands and canonical serialization.
- Every proposal includes the input hash, previous-state hash, configuration epoch, sequence number, policy version, software build identity, nonce, and expiry.
- Expiry is evaluated from a consensus-agreed logical time, block height, or timestamp rule rather than each replica's local wall clock.
- Loss of quorum stops final settlement. Read-only views must clearly report whether they are stale.

No single voter should decide the result. A central voter would merely create a new single point of failure.

### Quarantine and readmission

A node must not be expelled merely because it disagrees; an honest node can appear faulty during a network partition. Quarantine requires signed evidence such as an invalid state transition, conflicting signed statements, an invalid commit certificate, or a reproducible divergence from the deterministic specification. Membership changes require a specified joint or overlapping-quorum reconfiguration protocol committed across the old and new configuration epochs; an ordinary 5-of-7 application vote is not sufficient by itself.

A high-assurance recovery workflow could be:

1. Preserve the node's signed messages, logs, trace context, and build identity.
2. Quarantine it through the joint reconfiguration protocol.
3. Revoke its service credentials without destroying evidence.
4. Diagnose the incident using an independent reference implementation.
5. Reimage from a verified, reproducible build.
6. Restore a signed snapshot and replay the authoritative journal.
7. Run in non-voting shadow mode until results match over a defined observation window.
8. Re-admit through another joint, epoch-changing reconfiguration.

### Common-mode failure controls

Seven identical replicas can agree on the same incorrect answer. For the highest-assurance experiments, consider dissimilar redundancy:

- Multiple independently implemented execution engines following the same canonical protocol.
- Different runtime, compiler, operating-system, and database families where practical.
- A deliberately simple and slow reference ledger.
- An independently developed safety monitor that checks invariants rather than duplicating all business logic.
- Separate deployment pipelines, credentials, operators, and cloud or hardware failure domains.
- Cross-version and differential testing before membership changes.

Diversity introduces its own integration risks and does not fix incorrect requirements. Hazard analysis, specification review, formal models, and independent validation remain necessary.
